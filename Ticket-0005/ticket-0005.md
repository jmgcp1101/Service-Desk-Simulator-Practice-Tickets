Ticket Summary

Users across the entire 3rd floor were unable to access the internet, including conference rooms. The 2nd floor remained operational, indicating that the issue was isolated to the 3rd-floor network infrastructure. The outage was affecting multiple departments, including the Customer Service team, which could no longer access the CRM.

What I Did

Since the issue was affecting multiple users across an entire floor, I immediately treated it as a network infrastructure issue rather than an individual workstation problem.

I checked the server room and inspected the status of the 3rd-floor network switch. The switch was showing as offline, which explained why the devices connected to that floor were unable to reach the network.

I proceeded to reboot the switch. After the reboot, the switch came back online and internet connectivity was restored for the 3rd floor.

What I Did Wrong / Where I Got Stuck

Fortunately, I didn't encounter much difficulty with this ticket. Rebooting the affected switch was my first troubleshooting step after identifying that it was offline, and it resolved the issue immediately.

The main thing I learned from this ticket was the importance of using the scope of an issue as a troubleshooting clue. Since an entire floor was affected while another floor was still working, checking the shared network infrastructure was more appropriate than troubleshooting individual computers.

Key Takeaway

When multiple users in the same physical area lose connectivity at the same time, check the shared network infrastructure—such as switches, access points, uplinks, or other network devices—before troubleshooting individual endpoints.
